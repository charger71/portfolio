Code:You — Statewide Rebrand & Brand System

In 2015, Code Louisville launched as a nonprofit workforce development program teaching Kentuckians to code. By 2021, the Commonwealth of Kentucky wanted to replicate the program statewide as Code Kentucky — same curriculum, same mentors, same model. The result was dual-branded everything: two websites, two brands, fragmented student experience, and an operational burden that grew with every cohort.

During a leadership brainstorm, I proposed a name that could hold everything: Code:You. The previous brands tied the program to one city or one state. Code:You centers on the only thing that never changes — the student. The name scales to any geography, any future program. And the colon does double duty: it reads as both programming syntax and a personal address — code: you.

Working with a non-profit budget meant the agency produced one direction — a techno-brutalist treatment that was technically clean but felt cold for a program designed to help real people change careers. The one thing worth keeping was buried in their exploration: the sideways C:Y mark. Read sideways, the letters become a face. The C is a smile, the colon becomes a pair of eyes, the Y is a punk hairdo. It's immediately friendly without being childish. That mark became the kernel; everything else got rebuilt around it — lockup, color, typography, sub-brand system, and the rules for how it all fit together.

And for anyone who looks closely: the logo winks. On the live site, watch it on the homepage for about twenty seconds.

With a unified brand came the chance to fix what had been broken for students from the start: the application experience. Previously, a prospective student had to know which program they were applying for before they could even begin — Code Louisville or Code Kentucky? JavaScript or UX? Most students didn't know, and that friction was turning people away. The new code-you.org consolidated everything: a single application flow that routes students to the right program on the backend (invisible to the applicant), eligibility checks that automatically surface support like free laptops and stipends, and one resource hub instead of two scattered ones.

I also helped form a small internal marketing team and rebuilt the organization's social media presence from a dormant baseline, focusing on consistency and authenticity over volume.

Real Estate Distilled

Branding for a new real estate conference launching in a category full of polished, interchangeable corporate events. The whole positioning was anti-fluff: speakers were asked to “truly distill their talks down,” focusing only on revenue-producing tactics attendees could put to work the next Monday. The identity needed to feel like that — direct, confident, distinct, with no decoration that wasn’t doing work.

I developed the full brand from scratch: logo system, color and typography, icons, and the brand guide that kept everything consistent across conference materials. The mark won AIGA Louisville’s The Show award in 2017 and was later featured in HeyDesign.com’s logo inspiration roundup.

Global Louisville Action Plan

Unveiled in December 2016, the Global Louisville Action Plan is a collaborative effort between the Mayor’s Office, regional chamber of commerce Greater Louisville Inc., and hundreds of community members. It outlines key strategies to attract, retain, and grow Louisville’s foreign-born population and workforce.

I designed the 12-page booklet that introduced the plan to the public — including the cover system, interior layout, and a full set of custom infographics that translated dense demographic and economic data into something a general audience could actually read. The visualizations cover population growth projections, workforce composition by industry, education attainment comparisons, business ownership stats, and the plan’s three-pillar strategy framework (Integrate, Empower, Attract). Credited as designer on page 11 of the published document.

Fuzzy Zoeller Golf

Two 18-hole, par-72 courses — Champions Pointe and Covered Bridge — designed in collaboration with Fuzzy Zoeller. By 2018, the existing website was holding the business back: a tables-based static HTML site with no CMS, no responsive layout, and a booking flow that was effectively unusable on a phone. Both courses lived on every page, which made it hard to learn anything about either one. And the most important thing a golfer comes to a course website to do — book a tee time — was the hardest thing to actually accomplish.

I rebuilt the site from the ground up on WordPress with a custom theme, designed and developed end-to-end. Each course got its own clearly separated identity within the parent brand. New interactive course maps replaced the previous static layouts. Proper booking pages were built around the actual flow a golfer needs — pick a course, pick a date, pick a time — instead of the buried multi-step process the old site demanded.

After launch, online tee time bookings grew substantially and overall site traffic increased significantly. The courses themselves are still operating today — now managed by KemperSports under the combined name Covered Bridge and Champions Pointe.

The Work

  • Custom WordPress theme built from scratch
  • Responsive design (the original site was unusable on mobile)
  • Interactive course maps for both courses
  • Streamlined tee time booking flow
  • Replaced a tables-based static site with a CMS-driven build

The Natural Grip

The Natural Grip is a hand-protection product for cross-functional fitness — thin zinc oxide cotton tape, custom-fitted to ring-finger size, more breathable than weightlifting gloves and less bulky than wraps. In the fall of 2014, the company landed a featured spot on ABC’s Shark Tank, which gave us roughly six weeks to get the brand and site ready for a national-broadcast audience hitting all at once.

I led design and strategy as the senior member of the team, owned the design end-to-end, and co-developed with a dev partner to split the build under the deadline. The work covered two new branded landing pages targeted to the Shark Tank audience, refreshed custom illustrations, a rebuilt e-commerce ordering flow, SEO work in partnership with the agency’s search team, and hosting/infrastructure prep with the agency’s ops team for the traffic spike. The episode aired November 7, 2014.

  • 65,000+ visits to the Natural Grip site between November 7th–10th — more visits in 4 days than the previous year combined.
  • Year-over-year traffic up 5,000%.
  • Average session duration during the Shark Tank window: nearly six minutes (a 500% increase over the same period the previous year).
  • 20% of visitors reached the shop page during the campaign window.
  • Traffic was driven primarily by organic search (52% — viewers searching for "Natural Grip" during and after the show) and referral coverage of the episode.
  • A Yahoo! Finance article covering the episode drove an additional 16,000 visits to the site.

The broader campaign — including a video series produced by the agency’s video team using design assets from the brand work — went on to win four American Advertising Federation Louie Awards in 2015, including Judges’ Choice for the online video campaign.

The Work

  • Brand and visual design
  • Two branded landing pages
  • Custom illustrations
  • E-commerce ordering flow
  • Front-end development (with a dev partner)
